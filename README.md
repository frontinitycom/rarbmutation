# Cure MCOPS12 Project

Build a complete, production-ready multi-page website for "Cure MCOPS12" — an Austrian nonprofit organization dedicated to finding a cure for MCOPS12 (Syndromic Microphthalmia 12), an ultra-rare neurological disease. The primary goal of this website is to drive donations. Design inspiration: savingsasha.org — clean, emotional, modern, trust-building. NOT a generic nonprofit template.

DESIGN SYSTEM

Color palette: Deep navy blue (#1a2e4a) as primary, warm white (#fafaf8) as background, soft teal (#2a9d8f) as accent, amber (#e9c46a) for CTAs/donate buttons
Typography: Inter or similar clean sans-serif. Large emotional headlines, readable body text
Style: Modern medical/scientific nonprofit. Clean whitespace. Subtle gradient backgrounds. Real photos (placeholders where noted). NO stock-photo feel.
Navigation: Sticky top navbar with pill shape. Logo left. Nav links center. "Donate" button right — amber colored, always visible
Animations: Subtle scroll-fade-in animations on sections. No flashy effects.
Mobile: Fully responsive

PAGE 3: WHAT IS MCOPS12?
Section 1 — Overview
Headline: "What is MCOPS12?"

Syndromic Microphthalmia 12 (MCOPS12) is an ultra-rare neurodevelopmental disorder caused by mutations in the retinoic acid receptor beta (RARB) gene. First described in scientific literature in 2013, the most recent systematic review (2023) compiled clinical data from 52 individuals worldwide — making MCOPS12 one of the rarest disorders known. Because many children may be misdiagnosed with cerebral palsy or an unspecified neurodevelopmental disorder, the true number of affected individuals is likely higher.

Key Facts box (styled callout):

Ultra-rare neurodevelopmental disorder caused by RARB gene mutations
Also referred to as RARB-related disorder
Most frequent mutation: p.R387C (found in ~1/3 of patients)
Common features: eye defects, hypotonia, progressive dystonia/spasticity, language delay, feeding difficulties
Severity varies widely between patients
No approved treatment currently exists


Section 2 — The Gene
Headline: "The RARB Gene"

The retinoic acid receptor beta (RARB) gene is located on chromosome 3 (3p24.2). It encodes the RARβ protein — a nuclear receptor and transcription factor activated by retinoic acid (a metabolite of vitamin A). RARβ regulates the expression of many genes, playing a pivotal role in the development of the eyes, diaphragm, heart, digestive tract, and brain.

Section 3 — The Disease
Headline: "The Disease"

MCOPS12 is usually an autosomal dominant condition — a mutation in just one copy of the RARB gene is enough to cause the disorder. In most families, the gene change happens spontaneously (de novo variant). The mutated RARβ protein causes impaired transcription of genes in various target cells — affecting eye development, the diaphragm, heart, and critically, the striatum and prefrontal cortex in the brain, which explains the movement disorders and cognitive impairment seen in patients.

PAGE 6: RESEARCH & DRUG DEVELOPMENT
Section 1 — Two Paths to Treatment
Headline: "Our Path to Treatment"
Path 1 — Drug Repurposing:

Six commercially available drugs or drugs in clinical trials for other neurological diseases are being tested in a MCOPS12 mouse model to find treatments for hypotonia, dystonia, and cognitive impairments.

Path 2 — RNA Therapeutics (ASO/siRNA):

We are developing an antisense oligonucleotide (ASO) therapy to silence the mutated RARB mRNA, suppressing production of the dysfunctional RARβ protein while preserving the healthy copy. This approach is reversible and allele-specific.
